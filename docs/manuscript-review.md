## 🗂️ Categories & execution order

editorial decisions change the English, the English gates audio + translation, so
work top-down:

   register (greed/hatred/delusion vs wanting/anger/confusion), the refrain
   article ("just a/the body"), craving vs grasping, "What four?"/"Which four?",
   foot-washing wording. → see Open decisions + Editorial.
2. **English text edits.** Small wording fixes — some done (the Buddha addressed /
   Oxford "and" / cut "in it" / Pāli), the rest fall out of the decisions above.
3. **Translation / against-the-Pāli review → then zh propagation.** Word-checks
   ("former", "unsurpassed", "cry", "mine", "gurus", criteria #5/#6, "contents of
   experience", "Now Run It the Other Way"). Lock EN, then propagate to zh + zh audio.
4. **Audio batch.** Mechanism = post-pass silence. Covers: pauses/pacing, glitch
   re-records, title-order flip, preface-before-ch.1 (staged), 1.5s gaps (staged),
   the missing **Closing** track, colophon parity. → rebuild M4B → upload.
5. **PDF/print batch (blocked on `xelatex`).** Front matter / "contact sheet",
   drop epigraphs (likely just a stale printout), subtitle glosses, Pāli (done).

#### (superseded) Pause/pacing mechanism — DECIDED: post-pass silence insertion
Keep the Theo Silk `eleven_multilingual_v2` voice. Add pauses as real silence,
keyed off `[...]` markers in the **canonical MDX**.

**What already exists:** the *audio* pipeline already strips `[...]` tags
(`generate-audio.ts` callElevenLabsTTS, v2 branch) and already *injects*
`[long pause]` after every section heading. **What's missing:** the **web** render

would print on the site and in the book.

**Implementation plan (audio batch):**
1. Marker convention in canonical MDX: `[pause]` (≈1s) for mid-sentence spots;
   optional `[pause:1.5]` for a custom length. Mid-sentence example: "wanting to
   keep existing `[pause]` wanting to stop existing."
2. **Auto-silence at paragraph + heading breaks** (no marker needed) — covers most
   "pause before each paragraph / end of paragraph" notes automatically.
3. `generate-audio.ts`: for v2, **split** each section at markers/paragraph
   boundaries, synthesize the chunks, stitch with silence (reuse the chapter-gap
   silence helper). Stop *deleting* the tags.
4. Add a one-line `[...]` strip to the **web loader** (`src/content/index.ts` /
   a tiny remark plugin) and to **`book-source.ts` `readSuttaBody`** so markers
   never render.
5. "Too fast / odd tone" spots (e.g. "Getting sick is hard", "Where") get
   re-synthesized as part of the same pass; slower atempo or chunk boundaries
   smooth them.

#### Pacing recipe for "too-fast" lists — FINALIZED (first-talk triplets, by ear)
**For imperative micro-lists** (recognize/understand/know type): restructure into
**short capitalized imperatives separated by periods** — "Recognize it. Understand
it. Know it." The periods give ElevenLabs natural sentence breaks (good spacing),
the capitals give clean word attacks, and it reads as *intentional* in print (no
markers, no lone-capital weirdness). **At the normal −20% book rate — no per-section
slowdown needed** (the periods carry the spacing; −23% felt too slow). Applied to
`first-talk.mdx:57-60`, lead-in→list transition confirmed good.
- Key learning: **spacing ≠ rate.** The "too fast list" problem was *spacing*, and
  punctuation structure fixed it without dragging the tempo.
**For descriptive lists** (keep the words, audio-only breaths) — all LOCKED by ear:
- *Sense-bases* (fire-sermon): break the rushed phrase, e.g. "eyes, sights,
  `{/* pause:0.2 */}` and everything that follows: `{/* pause:0.2 */}` on fire." —
  apply to all six lines. (Garble was "and everything that follows" running into
  "on fire"; two 0.2s breaths fix it.)
- *Inline lists* (seven factors): a `{/* pause:0.2 */}` between each item — confirmed
  good for "investigation/energy".
- *Metta bullets* / single descriptive lines at normal rate read clean; the
  sequence just needs a small inter-item `{/* pause:0.3 */}`.
- **Normal −20% book rate throughout** — no per-section slowdown needed anywhere
  we've tested.

## 🧐 Curiosities (the user asked "why is this key" — answer together, not edits)
- **The cosmic ending of the First Talk** (gods crying out, the universe shaking,
  the light) — kept deliberately (the subtitle says "its literal cosmic ending
  kept in"). In the Pali it marks that this isn't an ordinary teaching: the
  *Dhammacakka* — the wheel of Dhamma — has been set turning, an irreversible
  world-event. The tiers of devas relaying the cry upward is the canon's way of
  saying the whole cosmos registered it.
- **"Koṇḍañña has understood" → Aññā Koṇḍañña ("Koṇḍañña Who Knows")** — the
  payoff of the whole sutta: the teaching *landed*. Koṇḍañña is the first person
  besides the Buddha to truly see it (stream-entry), so there are now two who
  know — the Dhamma is transmissible, not locked in one mind. He's renamed for
  that instant of seeing; "Aññā" = the direct knowing. It's the proof the wheel
  is turning.
