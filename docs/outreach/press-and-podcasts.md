# Press and podcast outreach — contacts, process, and what they change

Researched 9 Sep 2026. Companion to `docs/marketing-channels.csv` (fills its empty
`Link / contact` column) and to the Stage 0 "long-lead press + podcast pitches" row
in the staged launch plan.

**Verified** = read today on the target's own page, address copied verbatim.
**Unverified** = surfaced by search only, not confirmed at source.

## What this changes in the plan

Four findings, ordered by what they cost if ignored.

### 1. Lion's Roar will not accept an AI-drafted piece. Verbatim policy.

From `lionsroar.com/submissions/`, under "A Note on AI":

> As such we do not accept pieces generated primarily by AI.

They add that they use AI themselves "in limited ways: to support research and the
development of ideas, to streamline certain operational tasks, and in the creation of
some marketing language."

This does not kill the target, but it forces a distinction the pitch has to make in its
first line: **the book was AI-drafted; the article about it would not be.** A pitch that
opens with "AI-drafted plain-English sutras" reads as a submission their policy already
rejects. A pitch that opens with the editing method — weeks of line-by-line work against
the Pali, with the raw draft published beside the finished text as evidence — is a
human-written piece about AI, which the policy does not exclude.

The channels CSV rates Lion's Roar High leverage with no risk flag. Treat it as Med risk.

### 2. Lion's Roar, Buddhadharma, and lionsroar.com are one inbox.

`submissions@lionsroar.com` covers all three. The CSV and the staged plan carry them as
two rows with different lead times (4–8 weeks vs 8–12 weeks), both marked "Send NOW."
Two pitches into one inbox in the same week reads as spam. Send **one** pitch that names
the outlet you actually want and says you're happy to be redirected.

### 3. The Buddhistdoor series is closed, and its author already wrote the reply.

The staged plan's Stage 0 row proposes a reply to "So You Want to Be a Buddhist Author,
Part Five: AI," pitched to "that series editor." Three corrections:

- The author is **John Harvey Negru**, publisher at The Sumeru Press (Nepean, Ontario) —
  a contributing columnist, not a Buddhistdoor staff editor.
- Part Five ran **1 Sep 2025**. Part Six, "Bringing It All Back Home," ran **5 Nov 2025**
  and closed the six-part series by doing exactly what the pitch proposes: Negru went back
  to Daniel Aitken (Wisdom Publications), Nikko Odiseos (Shambhala Publications), and
  several teachers for their responses to the earlier installments, starting with AI.
  The reply already happened, a year ago, without you.
- Negru's own position is skeptical. Part Six's throughline is that Buddhism's survival
  depends on study and practice, "not the simulacrum of large language models," and he has
  written that he is "not hearing very much at all from Buddhist teachers about the dark
  side of artificial intelligence."

So the row's "Risk: Low" is wrong. Pitching Negru a reply to a closed series, from the
position he argued against, is the second-most adversarial target on the list after
Futurism — and unlike Futurism, it's currently on the send list.

The salvageable route is **Buddhistdoor's editorial team directly, as news, not as a
reply**. Precedent: BDG ran "Secular Buddhism Podcaster Launches AI Buddhist Learning
Platform" — Noah Rasheta, who is also on the podcast list, shipped an AI Buddhist product
and BDG covered it straight. They treat AI-and-dharma as a beat, not a scandal.

### 4. 10% Happier has rebranded. The URL in the CSV is stale.

`tenpercent.com` 301s to `meditatehappier.com`. The app and podcast are "Happier."
Old `@tenpercent.com` addresses still resolve for press and partnerships.

## Per target

### Tricycle — verified, but not by email

`editorial@tricycle.org` is confirmed on `tricycle.org/contact/`, alongside
`support@tricycle.org`, `learn@tricycle.org`, `foundation@tricycle.org`, and
`tricycle@gpr4ads.com` (advertising).

The catch: the contact page says electronic submissions only, **through an online form,
not email**. The form is referenced but not linked from that page, and
`tricycle.org/about/submissions/` 404s. Finding the live form is an open item.

Their stated process:

- A one-page query with the idea, your Buddhist and writing background, your familiarity
  with the subject, and clips or writing samples.
- Include word count, name, address, and the date.
- 500–2,000 words preferred; rarely over 4,000.
- Buddhism-related only. "Send work that reflects your knowledge and interests rather
  than trying to anticipate Tricycle's."
- **They only respond to submissions that have been accepted.** Silence is the no.

Plan implication: the CSV's 4–8 week lead time is really "no reply, ever, unless yes."
Don't hold Stage 6 for it.

### Lion's Roar and Buddhadharma — one pitch, and mind the AI policy

`submissions@lionsroar.com` for written work across Lion's Roar, Buddhadharma, and
lionsroar.com. Send an article draft or a pitch plus a short author bio. They try to
respond within eight weeks "but are not always able to do so."

They do not publish poetry, or work on yoga, life coaching, self-help, or general
spirituality, and they are not an academic publisher. Plus the AI policy in finding 1.

Art goes separately to Andrew Glencross — relevant here, because the illustrations are
CC0 and a Buddhadharma piece could run with them at no permissions cost. That is worth
one sentence in the pitch. Disclose that the art is AI-assisted if it is.

### Buddhistdoor Global — reroute from Negru to the editors

See finding 3. Automated fetching is blocked (403 to WebFetch and to curl with a browser
user agent); the pages read fine in a real browser, so check them by hand.

Two separable pitches, and they should not go out together:

1. **To BDG editorial**, as news or a feature: a free CC0 plain-English edition of six
   suttas, drafted with AI and reconciled line by line against the Pali, with the raw
   draft published beside the finished text. Their Rasheta coverage is the precedent.
2. **To Negru at Sumeru**, if at all, not as a pitch: as a publisher-to-publisher note
   from someone who read Part Six and is doing the thing he's skeptical about, in the
   open, with the evidence published. Ask for nothing. He may write about it; he may
   write against it. Both are survivable, neither is a launch dependency.

### Happier (ex-10% Happier) — no public guest route

No "suggest a guest" mechanism found on the current site. `press@tenpercent.com` is the
closest thing to a pitch address. Given three episodes a week and a large publicist
pipeline, treat this as the lowest-probability target on the list despite High leverage
in the CSV.

### Waking Up / Making Sense — one address, no stated policy

`media@samharris.org` for media and business inquiries; a general contact form and
`help.samharris.org` otherwise. No stated policy on unsolicited guest pitches, which
means no stated prohibition either. The CSV's "long shot but the AI angle is genuinely
his territory" still holds.

### Secular Buddhism (Noah Rasheta) — moved, and he built an AI product

`secularbuddhism.com` 301s to `eightfoldpath.com/sbp`. No email published anywhere on
the site. Routes: the "Get in Touch" form at `eightfoldpath.com/help` (name, email,
message up to 2,000 characters), a `/chat` AI assistant, and a `/community`.

The angle upgrade: Buddhistdoor reported that Rasheta launched an AI Buddhist learning
platform. He is not a target you have to persuade about AI — he is the one target on the
list who has already taken the same risk. Lead with that, not with Kālāma.

The CSV calls him "more reachable than the big two." Agreed, and now for a better reason.

### Deconstructing Yourself (Michael Taft) — no address, three weak doors

No email or contact page published. What exists: a public comment thread at
`deconstructingyourself.com/resources/feedback` (Taft replies there himself), an
anonymous feedback form, and a 1:1 coaching page that presumably routes somewhere.
None is a pitch channel. Low effort in the CSV is optimistic — finding a real route is
the work here.

### Guru Viking — contact form only

`guruviking.com/contact/` has a form and no published address: "You are welcome to get in
touch if you have any feedback, thoughts, or questions about the Guru Viking Podcast or
Meditation Club." No guest-pitch instructions. The making-of angle in the CSV fits the
show's long-form interview format.

### Buddhist Geeks — the only target that takes a finished asset

`buddhistgeeks.org` is a Substack ("Evolving Dharma," 9,000+ subscribers). Hosts are
Vince Fakhoury Horn and Emily Horn. No contact address published.

The important difference: the **community podcast series accepts finished recordings from
anyone** — the stated bar is the production skills to pull off the recording and the
connections to find a suitable guest. Host-side recording instructions (Zencastr) are at
`meta.buddhistgeeks.org/podcast/recording`; that page covers recording only and names no
submission destination.

This is the one row on the list where you can hand over a made thing instead of asking for
one. The audiobook and the illustrations already exist. Everywhere else on this sheet you
are pitching; here you are delivering. Worth reordering the effort accordingly.
