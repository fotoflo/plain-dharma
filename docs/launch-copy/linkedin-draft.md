One night in May I woke from vivid dreams at 3am and asked Claude "what did the Buddha actually say?"


Claude went and fetched the original Pali writings from SuttaCentral and proceeded to translate them.


I read "The Buddha's First Talk" in a sort of readable but recognisable AI slop language. Then, as a lark, I asked for it in Gen-Z speak. It was amazing and hilarious. But also slop. So I asked for it in plain modern English. And that worked - still a lot needed to be corrected, but I was hooked, reading till sunrise.


Then came the slow part. Weeks of editing them word by word, learning from Claude the different ways a word or phrase could be interpreted, and thinking of my children and friends and how they might read that English. I checked the text against existing translations (Bhikkhu Bodhi, Thanissaro Bhikkhu, SuttaCentral), and that only made me want to publish this more - they were too opaque for a lay audience like me, rather than friendly and kind, like the Buddha must have been.


Probably many scholars will object, but I just want it to be readable. Conversational. Fun.


The weeks also became an effort to make it more shareable - a website with an audio player, a mobile app, a book on Amazon. Today I'm excited to share it all for free (except the paperback, which Amazon charges for) on the website.


It's called Plain Dharma, the Buddha's six foundational teachings in modern English. The text, the audio and the art are all Creative Commons Zero: free to download, distribute, cut up, and remix. The cover reads "Translated by Claude Opus, edited by Alex Miller", because that's what happened.


Claude and I also made an audiobook (with ElevenLabs), and I loved it so much I started using it to put my kids to sleep. They now say they can't sleep without it, and I had to add a loop to the mobile app so it would play all night.


So please give it a read, or a listen. It's free. Each of the chapters is short, a few minutes each. I highly recommend listening to the Metta Sutra as a sort of guided meditation. I'll leave the link in the first comment.
