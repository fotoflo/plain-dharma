Excited to share the first printed copy of my new book Plain Dharma!

Before writing this, I'd never read a word the Buddha actually said, even though I studied Buddhism for years. At one point I even decided Buddhism was BS because I felt we must take action in the world and not simply give up our attachment to it to achieve a selfish freedom from suffering. Then years later came I back to it - learning to meditate and even praying at temples occasionally. So finally publishing the Buddha's own sermons is a nice circle.

One night in May, after coming back from a trip to the Beijing Auto Show, Guangzhou, and Dhaka (seeing the past and the future), I woke from vivid dreams at 3am and asked Claude "what did the buddha actually say?".

And Claude went and fetched the original Pali writings - Dhammacakkappavattana Sutra from SuttaCentral - and proceeded to translate them.

I read them first in a sort of readable but recognisable AI Slop language. Then, as a lark, I asked for the writings in Gen-Z speak. It was amazing and hilarious. But also slop. So I asked for them in plain modern English. And that worked. I was hooked, reading till sunrise.

There was slop there, but it was mostly great writing, storytelling, and a philosophy that was compelling - meaning that came before organised religion.

When I woke up again in the early afternoon, I was still captivated by what I remembered, and I decided that these writings had to be published - the original translations were too opaque to read by a lay audience (like me), but the core messages were clear as day, and the new translations brought them to light.

So I worked non-stop for a few weeks - translating them as The Buddha's First Talk, the Second Talk on not-self, the Fire Sermon, Loving-Kindness, the Foundations of Mindfulness, and How to Decide What to Believe, and editing them word by word, all the time learning from Claude the different ways a word or phrase could be interpreted, and thinking of my children and friends and how they might read that English - and also digesting and redigesting the Buddha's words.

I checked the text against existing translations (Bhikkhu Bodhi, Thanissaro Bhikkhu, SuttaCentral), the site links to them with a side-by-side comparison on the individual sutta pages. I don't expect it to be seen as perfect but hope it reads as lovely. Probably many scholars will object, but I just want it to be readable. Conversational. Fun - and if that brings more people into a philosophy of loving kindness and self understanding, that's the goal.

When that was done, I built a bit of a simple website and mobile app around them, and an audiobook with ElevenLabs narration which was also fantastic to listen to. I loved it so much that I started using it to put my kids to sleep and they started saying they can't sleep without it and I had to add a loop to the mobile app so it would play all night.

I also worked with my mother Ellen Shapiro, as well as Claude and Gemini to make the cover and website art. Mom even graciously donated the ISBNs from her big pack (mom's she's an author herself) so it wouldn't have to be on an Amazon ISBN.

Next, I published the texts and audio under a Creative Commons Zero licence on https://plaindharma.com - free to download, distribute, cut up, and remix (there's some great audio in there too, I'm hoping some DJs might sample it).

I also put it on Amazon (link in the first comment) for people who want a printed copy (or who want to support me and my work).

This weekend I got my first photo via WhatsApp from a friend who bought it, which was quite exhilarating. I haven't seen a print copy myself yet, but I'm so looking forward to it.

If you read it please comment your thoughts. If you're in Chiang Mai and want a copy, say so and I'll put one aside after I print them. And a question for everyone: I still have the Gen-Z version. Should I publish it?
