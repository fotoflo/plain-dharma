## Post 1  (366 chars)

One night in May, after coming back from a trip to the past and the future, I woke from vivid dreams at 3am and asked Claude "what did the buddha actually say?"

We argued for a while about what to read and finally Claude went and fetched the original Pali of Dhammacakkappavattana Sutra - The Buddha's first talk after enlightenment - and proceeded to translate it.

---

## Post 2  (470 chars)

I read it first in a sort of readable but recognizable AI Slop language. Then, as a lark, I asked for the writings in Gen-Z speak. It was amazing and hilarious. But also slop. So I asked for it in plain modern English. And that worked. I was hooked, and asked for the next one and the next one, reading till sunrise.

There was slop there, but it was mostly great writing, storytelling, and a philosophy that was compelling - meaning that came before organized religion.

---

## Post 3  (495 chars)

When I woke up again in the early afternoon, I was still captivated by what I remembered, and I decided that these writings had to be published - the original translations were too opaque to read by a lay audience (like me), but the core messages were clear as day, and the new translations brought them to light.

Previously, work like this would have been impossible. Pali is a 2500 year old dead language. Works like this were restricted to monks and scholars with Ph.Ds in classical Buddhism

## Post 4  (455 chars)

I checked the text against existing translations (Bhikkhu Bodhi, Thanissaro Bhikkhu, SuttaCentral), the site links to them with a side-by-side comparison on the individual sutra pages. I don't expect it to be seen as perfect but hope it reads as lovely. Probably many scholars will object, but I just want it to be readable. Conversational. Fun - and if that brings more people into a philosophy of loving kindness and self understanding, that's the goal.
